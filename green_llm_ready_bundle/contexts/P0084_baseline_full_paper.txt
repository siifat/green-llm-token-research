TITLE: Carbon-Aware Scheduling of Generative AI Inference
AUTHORS: Lee, H.; Gomez, R.; Nair, V.
YEAR: 2026

ABSTRACT
Generative AI services can shift flexible workloads across time or regions with different grid carbon intensity. We propose a carbon-aware scheduler that delays non-urgent batch inference within user-defined deadlines. Trace-driven simulation across three cloud regions reduced estimated carbon emissions by 21% with a median added delay of 7.4 minutes for flexible jobs, while real-time requests were unaffected.

1. INTRODUCTION
The environmental impact of AI inference depends not only on the amount of electricity consumed but also on when and where that electricity is used. Existing serving systems primarily optimize latency, throughput, and cost. This leaves a gap for workloads such as document summarization, batch grading assistance, and overnight report generation that can tolerate short delays.

2. RELATED WORK
Prior green-computing work has studied geographic load shifting for datacenters and carbon-aware scheduling for general cloud jobs. LLM serving research focuses on batching and accelerator utilization. Few systems combine inference deadlines, regional carbon intensity, and model availability in one scheduling policy.

3. METHODS
We replayed 30 days of synthetic inference requests based on a university AI platform. Jobs were labeled real-time, 15-minute-flexible, or 2-hour-flexible. Carbon intensity traces were sampled at five-minute intervals for three regions. The scheduler selected the lowest-carbon feasible execution slot subject to deadline, capacity, and model-residency constraints. Baselines were immediate execution and cost-only routing.

4. RESULTS
Compared with immediate execution, the proposed scheduler reduced estimated emissions by 21%. The cost-only baseline reduced cost by 8% but emissions by only 3%. Real-time requests had no added delay. Flexible jobs experienced a median delay of 7.4 minutes and a 95th percentile delay of 38 minutes.

5. DISCUSSION
Carbon-aware delay is suitable only for workloads with explicit flexibility. The simulation assumes accurate carbon-intensity forecasts and does not account for embodied hardware emissions. Regional data movement may also create privacy or compliance constraints.

6. CONCLUSION
Deadline-aware carbon scheduling can reduce operational emissions without degrading latency-sensitive requests when some inference demand is flexible.

7. FUTURE WORK
Future work should incorporate forecast uncertainty, water intensity, and real production traces.

APPENDIX A. REPRODUCIBILITY AND REPORTING NOTES

The experimental workflow was logged from data preparation through analysis so that the main comparisons could be reconstructed. Random seeds, model or software versions, preprocessing decisions, and evaluation settings were fixed before the final run. Pilot executions were used only to verify the pipeline and were not mixed with the reported measurements. Intermediate outputs were retained rather than overwritten so that anomalous observations could be traced to their source.

The authors also recorded several contextual variables that were not central to the primary research question. These included runtime environment information, routine validation checks, and descriptive summaries used to detect data-entry or processing mistakes. Such details improve auditability but are not necessary for understanding the headline result. Where measurements depended on hardware or institutional conditions, the paper treats absolute values as setting-specific and emphasizes the relative comparison made under matched conditions.

For replication, future investigators are advised to preserve the same task definitions while independently re-running the analysis on different hardware, datasets, or student populations. The paper distinguishes direct measurements from derived estimates and avoids treating a reduction in one proxy metric as an identical reduction in environmental impact. This distinction is important because energy, latency, carbon intensity, and model quality can change at different rates.
